How to play

Goal

Work out where everyone was at the moment of the murder. The murderer is the one person who was in the same room as the victim, with nobody else there.

Rules

  • Exactly one person per row and per column.
  • Nobody stands on blocking furniture (shown as solid gray tiles: tables, shelves, plants, trees…). Beds, chairs, sofas, carpets, cars and similar can be occupied.
  • A piece of furniture can cover several squares, but a person only takes one of them.
  • The victim is a person too: they count for rows, columns and every clue.

Controls

  • Tap a person (chip or statement card) to select them.
  • Tap a square: add or remove a small note for that person.
  • Press & hold a square: place the person there (hold again to remove).
  • Drag: paint notes across several squares.
  • ✕ Mark: tap or drag to rule squares out. Eraser: tap to clear a square, hold the button to clear the board.

Nobody else was in that room, not even the victim.
alone with
Only those two people were in the room.
nobody was in…
That room was completely empty.
corner
A square where a horizontal and a vertical wall of its room meet.
same diagonal
On either diagonal line through the other person, across the whole grid.
same row / column
The whole line across the grid, not just the room.
west / east of
Any square in a column to the left / right, in any row.

1. Look for forced squares

If a row or column has only one open square, somebody is there, even if you don't know who yet.

2. Pairs lock lines

If two people are each confined to the same two columns, nobody else can use those columns. Same for rows.

3. Cross the intersections

If a person can only be in a few squares, any square that shares a row or column with all of them is ruled out for everyone else.

4. Work backwards

Find a square that must be filled, then ask who could possibly be there.

Does “someone” include the victim?

Yes. Think of the scene just before the murder, while the victim was still alive.

Are the statements always true?

Yes. No tricks, no lies.

“Beside a chair”: could it be several chairs?

Yes, at least one. A clue would say “exactly one” if the number mattered. Someone beside a chair may also sit on a chair.

Is there exactly one solution?

Yes, every case is checked by a solver. If you found another, re-read the clues carefully, and remember the victim must be alone with the murderer.

Do I ever have to guess?

No. Every case can be solved by deduction.

Do the names matter?

Only the letters. Names are cosmetic.
